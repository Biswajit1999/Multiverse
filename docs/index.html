THE SHORT ANSWER

If I had to leave one word for humanity, it would be inflation.

Not because every alternative has been disproved, and not because inflation explains why anything exists at all. The reason is narrower and testable: an inflationary phase can dynamically precede the thermal Big Bang, generate primordial fluctuations, and end through reheating into a hot radiation bath.

That chain connects a pre-hot-Big-Bang mechanism to measurements we can actually make: the scalar spectral index ns, the tensor-to-scalar ratio r, the CMB power spectrum, curvature and non-Gaussianity.

My working conclusion is not “the multiverse exists.” It is: inflation + reheating is the most productive, testable route currently available for explaining how the hot Big Bang could have emerged.

FIELD NOTE 001 — WRITTEN FROM 2026, WITH A THOUSAND-YEAR HORIZON

Imagine we are the archaeologists of spacetime.

We do not stand outside the universe and photograph its birth. We inherit relics: a microwave background, a distribution of galaxies, light-element abundances, gravitational-wave limits and the equations that connect those fossils to possible earlier dynamics.

The scientifically useful move is to stop asking only, “Which theory sounds most profound?” and instead ask, “Which prior state evolves into our hot universe and leaves the fingerprints we actually observe?”

For this project, that filter changes the story. Eternal inflation, quantum tunnelling, cyclic histories and nonsingular bounces remain intellectually serious, but they sit at different distances from measurement. Inflation is not chosen because it is dramatic. It is chosen as the working answer because it currently gives the cleanest bridge from pre-hot-Big-Bang dynamics to a calculable primordial spectrum and reheating history.

The most important unresolved step is therefore not another conceptual image. It is the next discriminant: a prediction that a competing origin model cannot mimic.
