What alternatives exist to an inflationary prehistory?

A bounce, a cyclic universe and a quantum boundary are very different proposals. They should be compared by mechanism, perturbations and observables rather than by visual similarity.

Chapter 06 · authored by Biswajit Jana · updated 2 October 2026

1. Nonsingular bounce2. Ekpyrotic and cyclic histories3. Quantum cosmology4. How to compare fairly5. Why the question remains open

Nonsingular bounce

A bounce replaces a classical singular transition with contraction reaching a minimum scale before re-expansion. In effective loop quantum cosmology one often encounters H² = (8πG/3)ρ(1−ρ/ρ_c), which forces H to vanish at a critical density. The central questions are whether the effective description remains reliable and whether perturbations pass consistently through the bounce.

Ekpyrotic and cyclic histories

Ekpyrotic scenarios use a slowly contracting phase with a stiff effective equation of state to suppress anisotropy and generate a smooth background. Cyclic models attempt repeated phases of contraction and expansion. They must control entropy production, instabilities and the transition between phases while still explaining the observed primordial perturbations.

Bounce example: H² = (8πG/3)ρ(1 − ρ/ρ_c). Quantum boundary: ĤΨ = 0.

Quantum cosmology

Quantum cosmology asks whether the origin problem should be described by a wave function and boundary condition rather than by an earlier classical epoch. In minisuperspace, a Wheeler–DeWitt equation takes a schematic form such as [−d²/da² + U(a)]Ψ(a)=0. No-boundary and tunnelling proposals impose different conditions on Ψ.

How to compare fairly

Each model should be forced through the same sequence: define degrees of freedom; evolve the background; calculate perturbations; reach a hot radiation-dominated state; predict observables; compare with data; identify assumptions that cannot yet be tested. A model is not stronger merely because it avoids the word “beginning.”

Why the question remains open

Current observations constrain classes of early-universe physics but do not uniquely reconstruct the pre-hot-Big-Bang history. The most useful competition is therefore between explicit predictive models, not between broad labels.

What to remember

Evidence boundaryThis chapter distinguishes direct observations from calculations and model-dependent extrapolation. A physically possible scenario is not automatically an observed history.
