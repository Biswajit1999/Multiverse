How does the CMB test inflation?

Inflation is useful only if an early-universe model connects to measurable relics. The cosmic microwave background is the cleanest bridge between primordial perturbations and precision data.

Chapter 03 · authored by Biswajit Jana · updated 2 October 2026

What the CMB measures

The cosmic microwave background is relic radiation released when the universe became transparent about 380,000 years after the hot Big Bang. Tiny temperature and polarization variations across the sky encode density, velocity and gravitational-potential perturbations in the early plasma.

Why the acoustic peaks matter

Before recombination, photons and baryons formed a coupled fluid. Gravity compressed overdense regions while radiation pressure resisted compression, producing acoustic oscillations. The pattern of peak positions and heights in the angular power spectra constrains geometry, baryon density, dark matter density, the primordial spectrum and other cosmological parameters.

The inflation connection

Inflationary quantum fluctuations provide a mechanism for generating a nearly scale-invariant, approximately Gaussian, predominantly adiabatic primordial spectrum. The scalar spectral index nₛ measures departure from exact scale invariance. The tensor-to-scalar ratio r quantifies a possible primordial gravitational-wave contribution. Different inflationary potentials trace different locations in the nₛ–r plane.

What the public data can and cannot do

NASA LAMBDA provides plotting tables and published spectra from experiments such as Planck, ACT and SPT. These are excellent for visual comparison and education. A full cosmological parameter analysis requires the experiment likelihoods, covariance matrices, foreground models, calibration uncertainties and nuisance parameters; a plotted bandpower table is not a replacement for that machinery.

What would be especially important

A convincing detection of primordial B modes with a consistent frequency dependence and foreground treatment would be a major clue about inflationary energy scales. Stronger upper limits still matter because they exclude classes of models. Better measurements of non-Gaussianity, running and primordial features provide additional ways to distinguish early-universe histories.

What to remember

Evidence boundaryThis chapter distinguishes direct observations from calculations and model-dependent extrapolation. A physically possible scenario is not automatically an observed history.
