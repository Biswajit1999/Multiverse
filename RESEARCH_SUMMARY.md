# Research Summary — What is outside the observable universe, and what preceded the hot Big Bang?

**Author: Biswajit Jana**

The original intuition behind this project was a room with walls: if our observable universe is the room, what exists after the walls are removed? Modern cosmology changes the analogy. The boundary we observe is not known to be a physical wall. It is primarily a **causal boundary** set by the finite age and expansion history of spacetime.

The project therefore separates two questions.

**1. What lies beyond our observable horizon?** Standard FLRW cosmology can contain spatial regions outside our present particle horizon without requiring a multiverse. They are simply causally inaccessible today.

**2. Could there be physically separate cosmological regions?** Some false-vacuum eternal-inflation models contain distinct reheating bubbles, potentially with different low-energy vacuum states. This is a stronger and more model-dependent statement.

The repository now calculates both the causal structure of an FLRW universe and the field dynamics that can lead to inflation, reheating, vacuum decay and quantum-cosmology boundary proposals.

## Main findings from the implemented models

At $N=60$, the repository obtains approximately $r=0.132$ for a quadratic potential but $r=0.003$ for a Starobinsky-type plateau. The current observational upper bound used here is $r<0.034$ in the cited 2026 synthesis, so the simple quadratic case is strongly disfavoured by this compressed diagnostic while the plateau model remains below the tensor limit.

Vacuum-decay calculations show why "bubble universe" imagery must be tied to an actual potential and Euclidean solution. The relevant object is an $O(4)$ instanton with scalar and gravitational backreaction; thin-wall and Hawking–Moss formulas provide controlled limits, but not every potential admits the same bounce.

The Wheeler–DeWitt experiments show a different possibility: the origin question may be a quantum boundary-condition problem rather than a classical event occurring at an earlier time. The repository deliberately avoids turning semiclassical wave-function weights into literal probabilities without specifying an interpretation and measure.

## What the project does not establish

It does not establish that a multiverse exists, that our universe was created from nothing, or that one pre-Big-Bang scenario is uniquely correct.

The mature result is methodological: the vague origin question has been decomposed into equations, numerical experiments and observable discriminants that can be improved as theory and data advance.
