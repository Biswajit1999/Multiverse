# Singularity theorems and the past boundary

## Hawking-Penrose

The Hawking-Penrose theorem establishes geodesic incompleteness under a set of assumptions involving causal structure, focusing, and energy conditions.

The important distinction is:

**geodesic incompleteness ≠ proof of creation from nothing.**

A geodesically incomplete classical spacetime may indicate that the theory has reached the edge of its domain of validity.

## Borde-Guth-Vilenkin

A widely discussed result applies to spacetimes whose expansion rate is positive on average along a past-directed geodesic. Schematically,

$$
H_{\rm av}>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{past geodesic incompleteness}.
$$

It does not use the same energy-condition assumptions as many classical singularity theorems.

The implication for inflation is precise: inflation by itself cannot be assumed to provide a complete description arbitrarily far into the past whenever the theorem's condition applies.

It does **not** uniquely select a singular beginning, tunneling creation, a bounce, or a no-boundary state. Additional physics is required.
